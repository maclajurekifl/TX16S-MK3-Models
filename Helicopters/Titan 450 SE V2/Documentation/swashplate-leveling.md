# Swashplate levelling

We needed a lot of **positive cyclic trim** on pitch and roll. That usually means the swash is tilted at “center,” not that the radio should carry big trim forever.

## Tool

A 3-leg swash leveler on the **5 mm** main shaft (fits this 450). Slide over the shaft; feet sit on the outer swash ring.

## Goals

- Cyclic trim / subtrim near **0**
- Swash **level** at mid collective (hover setup)
- Servo arms ~**90°** to links at that center

## Steps

### 1. Zero the radio cyclic
Aileron/elevator trim and subtrim → 0. No leftover cyclic mixes.

### 2. Level at mid stick
1. Collective at mid (hover setup position), cyclic centered  
2. Leveler on shaft  
3. Adjust the **three swash links** until all feet sit with no rock/gap  

### 3. Check it isn’t only perfect at mid
Briefly check low and high collective (cyclic still centered). Mid level matters most for hover; large tilt through travel means unequal geometry.

### 4. Servo arms
At mid, arms as close to **90°** as practical (horn spline / small link tweaks — not radio trim).

### 5. Flybar (this is a flybar 450)
- Seesaw roughly level at mid  
- Paddle links equal  
- Paddles aligned with each other  

### 6. Main blades
- Both grips same pitch at mid (pitch gauge if you have one)  
- Track blades after reassembly  

### 7. Hover check
Only **tiny** cyclic trim if needed. Still needs a lot → return to swash/arms.

## After levelling — order we used

1. Confirm level through a bit of travel  
2. Arms / flybar / blade match  
3. Short hop (2S OK for tune)  
4. Final feel on **3S**

There is **no FBL “level calibrate”** on a flybar machine — geometry is the calibration.
